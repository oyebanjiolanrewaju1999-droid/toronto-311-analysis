# Toronto 311 — one-page memo

**To:** 311 operations / Solid Waste Services
**From:** Philip Oyebanji
**Date:** 9 November 2026
**Re:** 1.35M requests, 2023–2025

## Finding 1 — Volume is up 21% in two years

414,282 requests in 2023, 437,043 in 2024, 500,269 in 2025. Two divisions
produced most of the increase: Solid Waste (+29.8%) and Transportation (+23.3%).
Municipal Licensing grew 9%. If budgets were set on 2023 volumes, the two growing
divisions are already running ahead of them.

## Finding 2 — The work is spread across hundreds of categories, and weather moves it

830 distinct request types, and the eight largest are only 19.3% of the total.
The biggest single category, *Injured – Wildlife*, is 3.8%. Any staffing model or
dashboard built around "the top 10 request types" is looking at a fifth of the
work.

Volume also isn't steady. February 2025 recorded 53,707 requests against a monthly
norm near 33,000 — 6,168 of them arrived on 19 February alone, almost all sidewalk
and road snow clearing. A plan built on average demand will be short-staffed the
week it matters most.

## Finding 3 — One in six Solid Waste requests is cancelled

76,987 cancellations, 16.9% of that division's volume, versus 2.5% for
Transportation. The five most-cancelled types are all missed-collection
complaints — garbage, furniture, recycling, green bin, yard waste — 36,079
requests in total. These are dispatches that generated no service.

## Recommendation

Treat the cancellation rate as the first optimisation target, not capacity. A
quarter reduction in Solid Waste cancellations is about **19,000 fewer wasted
dispatches** across three years — comparable to a meaningful capacity increase,
at a fraction of the cost. The first diagnostic step is cheap: group the
cancellations by address and by collection day to see whether they cluster on a
route or a repeat client.

## Caveat

Division-level trends are only reliable where the division name is stable. The
`Parks` division was renamed `Parks and Recreation` in 2025 and its recorded volume
fell from 34,203 to 1,437 — a labelling change, not a real decline. Any
year-over-year chart that includes Parks is wrong, and it would have looked like a
96% collapse without a note in the data.
